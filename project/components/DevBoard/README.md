# DevBoard

A microcontroller board template in two layouts, drawn at the real board's size. Provide `label`, `chipLabel`, `finish`, `pinKind` (`socket` for boards people plug wires into, `pad` for castellated dev kits) and `indicators` (`power`, `user`), then the rows for the layout.

- **Portrait** (default): `left` and `right` are pin names top to bottom, a whole number of pitches apart so the board sits in a breadboard. `controller: "chip"` draws the USB, regulator, crystal, chip and ICSP block, as on a Nano-class board; `controller: "module"` draws a shielded radio module with its antenna meander at the top, the USB and the `EN` and `BOOT` buttons at the bottom, as on an ESP32 dev kit.
- **Landscape** is the Uno-class board drawn from the real one, 68.58 × 53.34 mm: the outline with its chamfered top-right corner and the wider ICSP side, the four holes where the real ones are, the USB-B and the barrel jack 4 mm over the left edge, the reset button in the corner above the USB with the bridge chip's own ICSP block beside it, the bridge chip with its crystal, the `L`, `TX` and `RX` column under pin 13, `ON` by the ICSP block, the board's name where the real one prints its own, the DIP-28 at the bottom right with its crystal, the regulator and the two electrolytics by the jack. `top` and `bottom` are the header groups as the board reads them left to right with the USB on the left: `SCL … 8` then `7 … 0` along the top, the power group then `A0 … A5` along the bottom; the 0.16 inch gap between the digital headers is snapped to two pitches so every pin sits on the grid. `L`, `TX`, `RX` under the digital header and `ON` by the ICSP. `groupNames` prints the silk words by each group.
- Every header pin has a trace toward the controller and the board carries a few vias, placed clear of the pin labels and the ICSP block (a narrow board with long labels keeps one fewer); the landscape board also has the two electrolytic capacitors by its jack, and its DIP-28 shows its moulding and lot code.
- `teal` with sockets for Uno, Nano and Mega-class boards; `black` with pads and a module for ESP32 dev kits; `green` with pads for RP2040 boards; `white` for Nucleo boards.
- Each indicator in `indicators` is `true`, `false` or a level from 0 to 1 that dims its glow, for an LED a sketch drives with PWM; every board generator takes the same.
- Indicators use the editor's own hooks: `user-led` (lit from the board's LED pin current), `power-led` with `data-lit-state="power"`, and `tx-led`, `rx-led` with their states on the landscape board; a dev kit's addressable LED is `rgb-led`. Each carries `data-on-fill` and `data-off-fill`. The reset button is `data-hook="reset-button"`.
- No vendor name or logo on the silk: say what the board is (`UNO-CLASS`, `ESP32-C3`), never whose.
- A name that repeats down a row carries a `.N` suffix in `left` and `right` (`GND.2`), so every pin has its own name; the silk prints it without the suffix (`GND`), as the real board does, and the pin keeps the full name. `espDevKitTemplate`, `picoBoardTemplate`, `nucleo32BoardTemplate`, `nucleo64BoardTemplate`, `bluePillBoard` and `nanoConnectTemplate` print their names the same way.
- `pwm` names the top-row pins the board marks with a `~` (the Uno's 3, 5, 6, 9, 10 and 11); the mark prints before each label as on the silk.

- `labels` prints other silk for named pins, by pin name: the Uno's second A4 and A5 as `SDA` and `SCL`, a Nano's `12` as `D12`. `megaBoardTemplate`, `franzininho`, `espDevKitTemplate` and `nanoConnectTemplate` take it too.
- `icsp` (portrait, chip) makes the ICSP block six pins, named in the header's order (MISO, VCC, SCK, MOSI, RESET, GND): a 2 × 3 block at the far end between the rows, pins 2, 4 and 6 on the last row and 1, 3 and 5 a pitch beyond, pin 1 at the left in a square, as on a Nano-class board; the board grows a pitch to hold it. A portrait board under 8 pitches wide (a Nano at its real 7.2) has no room for the reset button beside the indicators, so the button sits under the controller.
- `topPitches` lowers the first row, `extraPads` adds named pads on the grid from the first pin, `resetLabel` prints the button. `controller: 'module'` draws a shielded radio module above the rows with the buttons and USB below them (`moduleSize: "wroom"` for the larger can, `usb`: `micro`, `c`, `dual-c` or `dual-micro`, `buttons` left to right), for boards of your own; Espressif's DevKitM-1 and DevKitC-1 boards are `espDevKitTemplate({ layout: 'devkit-1' })` at their true size.
- The Pico and the Nucleo-32 have their own generators, drawn from photographs: `picoBoardTemplate` (green 21 × 51 mm, castellated pads named inside, the micro USB and LED at the top, BOOTSEL at the left as `boot-button`, the RP2040 with its flash and crystal, the three debug pads at the bottom, which are pins unless `debugPins` is false, four holes) and `nucleo32BoardTemplate` (white 18.5 × 50.8 mm, the micro USB with COM and PWR lamps, the ST-LINK, the reset button, the target with its crystal, LD3 as `user-led`, the VCP pads on the grid). The Uno, Mega, Nano RP2040 Connect, Nucleo-64 and Franzininho were checked against photographs too: the Mega's ON lamp sits by its ICSP block with the crystal left of the controller and the name at the bottom right, the Nano Connect is navy, the Nucleo-64's USB sits right of centre with the USER and RESET buttons inboard, and the Franzininho is green with its two 3 mm lamps at the bottom centre and four holes.
