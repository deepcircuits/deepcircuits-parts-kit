The Parts Kit is how DeepCircuits draws electronic parts: the LEDs, chips, modules and boards people drop onto the workbench. One grid, one set of materials, one way to show state. A part drawn with it looks like the real thing on a bench, lines up on a breadboard, and reads on a light or dark canvas without changing its own paint. It is the rule book for every part added to it.

## What a part is here

- Draw boards, modules, chips and parts that lie flat **from directly above, at true scale**. Draw parts that stand on the bench (LEDs, radial capacitors, TO-92 sensors) **in front elevation, legs down**, the way they sit in a breadboard, so they read at a glance.
- **The bench light comes from the top left**, on every part. Paint is flat, with volume from two translucent overlays: a white light at `opacity-light` along the top or left of a cylinder or dome, a black shade at `opacity-shade` along the bottom or right. No shadows and no filters; gradients only as the five sheens below. One light direction across the whole kit is what makes the parts read as one bench rather than a pile of clip art. Boxes (chips, buttons, cans seen from above, module housings) take an L-shaped bevel from `PartsKit.bevel`: one light path along the top and left edges, one shade path along the bottom and right. Every board edge takes the same bevel from `PartsKit.bevelPoly`, which follows any outline (the Uno's chamfer, the Franzininho's USB tongue): edges facing up or left are lit, edges facing down or right shaded. Both bands are stroked along the body's own rounded outline, half their width inside it, so they turn each corner with the body instead of poking past it; pass the body's corner radius to `bevel`. Curved surfaces take a sheen on top of their flat colour, the kit's one use of gradients: `g-dome` on LED domes, `g-lens` on lenses and knob caps, `g-metal` on cans and metal faces, and on a lit LED `g-glow-<hex>` for the halo (one gradient per glow colour, so several lit LEDs on one page each keep their own colour) and `g-hot` for the white core over the die. The sheen is an overlay, so a state painter that resets a lens fill never loses it.
- **Detail is what the real part has**, drawn in the kit's materials: the lead frame inside an LED, the end caps of a resistor, the stripe on a capacitor, the knurl on a knob, the tabs on a button plate, the crystal and passives on a board, the traces under its mask and its vias, the lot code and moulding marks on a chip. Nothing decorative that the part does not carry.
- **Start from the real part.** Look at the physical part before drawing: its silhouette, its true size, and the features people recognise it by (the lead frame inside an LED, the stripe on an electrolytic, the two cans on an ultrasonic ranger, the USB-B and barrel jack on an Uno-class board). Draw those at true size (see *Measured from the real part*), then snap the pins to the grid and say where you snapped.
- **Original art only.** Never copy another simulator's or vendor's board art, documentation drawings or logos. Name what a board is on its silk (`UNO-CLASS`, `ESP32-C3`), never whose it is.
- A part has **a fixed material palette and a canvas-facing ink**. Its body is painted in the material tokens below and looks the same on any canvas. Anything that stands on the canvas rather than on the part (a chip's pin names, a logic symbol, an outline part) is `currentColor`, which is `canvas-ink` in either theme.
- **Presentation attributes only.** SVG sanitizers strip `<style>` and `style=""`, so every fill, stroke and font is an attribute. Give the root `<svg>` explicit `width`, `height`, a matching `viewBox`, `role="img"` and an `aria-label` that says what the part is and, if it has one, its state (`red LED, lit`).
- **Checked, not eyeballed.** Every generator's output passes `PartsKit.check`, the kit's overlap check: labels never touch each other or a part, parts never touch each other, nothing leaves the box. A part that fails the check does not ship, whatever it looks like at one zoom.
- Write a generator, not a file. A part is a function of its attributes that returns an `Artwork` (`width`, `height`, `svg`, `pins`); the kit's ES modules (`src/`) are the reference implementation, and `generators/bundle.js` (`window.PartsKit`) is built from them.

## The grid

- The unit is `pitch`: 0.1 inch is 9.6 px at 96 dpi, the breadboard's hole pitch. **Every pin of a part sits a whole number of pitches from its first pin.** `PartsKit.pinsOnPitch(pins)` is the test; a part that fails it does not ship.
- Size bodies in pitches (`pitch-half`, `pitch-2`, `pitch-3`) and round coordinates to 0.01 px. A DIP's rows are `pitch-3` apart so it straddles the breadboard channel; SOIC and TSSOP rows are `pitch-2`.
- Start a header `pin-margin` from the board edge and centre it: `x = (width − (n − 1) × pitch) / 2`. Two rows on one board are a whole number of pitches apart.
- Legs of a two-leg part are one pitch apart, at `pitch-half` and 1.5 × `pitch` inside a two-pitch-wide box.

## Materials

Use the token for the material, never a colour of your own. Each body has one paint and one darker edge token.

- **Boards** come in seven finishes, chosen by family: `pcb-blue` for display and sensor modules; `pcb-green` for RP2040-class boards and character LCDs; `pcb-teal` for Uno, Nano and Mega-class boards; `pcb-black` for ESP32 dev kits, instruments, the e-paper carrier and NeoPixel boards; `pcb-purple` for small sensor breakouts; `pcb-white` for Nucleo-class boards; `pcb-red` for the 2.4 inch TFT module and other red breakouts. Print on a dark finish is `silk`; on `pcb-white` it is `silk-dark`. Pads are `pad` with a `hole` centre ringed in `metal-edge`; mount holes are `hole` ringed in `pad`. Both show the canvas through the hole, as a drilled board does; dark openings that are not drilled through (sockets, mic ports, a jack's bore) are `cavity`. Traces run under the mask as white at 11% (black at 10% on `pcb-white`), 1.1px with round joins, from each pad toward the part it serves (`PartsKit.trace`); a via is a 1.2px ring of `pad` with a `hole` (`PartsKit.via`). Draw a few, where the real board routes them; never a dense maze.
- **Chips** are `chip` with a `chip-edge` stroke, `chip-print` for the part number, notch and pin numbers, and `lead` leads with a shoulder where they leave the body. The moulding shows as a light along one long edge and a shade along the other, and two ejector-pin rings at the ends. Every chip carries a lot code (`PartsKit.lotCode`: `DC` and five characters from the part number, never a vendor's) at `pin-label` size. On a bare chip it stands beside the part number, the two centred as one block that stays clear of a DIP's pin numbers; on a module's small package it is cut to the characters the body holds; where there is no room for it, it is left off.
- **Plastics**: `plastic-black` for button bodies, header housings, buzzer and pot cases and bezels; `plastic-grey` for knobs, switch bodies and display frames; `plastic-white` for sensor housings and connectors; `plastic-blue` for the DHT11's housing, the one sensor people know by its colour, and the keypad's number keys; `plastic-red` for the DIP switch and the keypad's letter keys; `plastic-amber` for the KS2E relay's can. **Metal** is `metal` for cans, USB shells, screw terminals and button plates; every USB receptacle is one drawing (`PartsKit.usbPort`: micro, mini, USB-C, with the USB-B on the Uno and Mega), the folded shell with its sheen and seam, the rear solder tabs, the latch windows and the dark mouth at the board's edge. `brass` is the tilt switch's gilded can.
- **Glass**: `glass` for an unlit display window inside a `bezel`; the run paints over it. A character LCD is `lcd-green` with `lcd-ink` characters or `lcd-blue` with `lcd-ink-light`. A seven-segment or matrix face is `segment-face` with `segment-off` elements. An instrument's own readout is `readout` with `readout-text`.
- **Passives**: an axial resistor is `resistor-body` with `band-*` bands from the colour code; a resistor on a module is `resistor-smd`. Capacitors are `cap-electrolytic` with a `cap-stripe`, `cap-ceramic`, or `cap-mlcc` for the brown chip capacitors on the small sensor boards. The breadboard is `bread-body`, `bread-hole`, `bread-print`, with `rail-plus` and `rail-minus` stripes.
- **Our mark.** Every board carries the probe on a node in its silk, where a real board prints its maker's mark (`PartsKit.silkMark`, one ink, the board's `silk`). The templates place it where there is room: a module from eight pitches wide, a dev board by its name, the LCD by its lower edge. It is the one thing a DeepCircuits board shows that no other drawing does, and it is ours to show.
- **Light** is a lens colour: `lens-red`, `lens-green`, `lens-yellow`, `lens-blue`, `lens-white`, `lens-orange`. Button caps use the same tokens. A part attribute may carry any plain CSS colour instead; `PartsKit.lensColor` accepts a token name or a colour and nothing else.

## Edges, strokes and corners

- **Every lead and leg is two-tone**: a `lead-edge` backing stroke at `lead-width` (2.9px, the Fritzing 0.03 inch) under a `lead` core 1px narrower, so a wire reads as round and keeps 3:1 against a white canvas. `PartsKit.wire` draws it; chip leads, TO-92 legs and header legs use the same pair.

- Every body gets one edge stroke of its `-edge` token at `stroke-outline`. Details inside a body (a leg's foot, a small part on a module) use `stroke-detail`; hairlines (band edges, cell gaps) use `stroke-fine`. Symbols in `currentColor` use `stroke-symbol`.
- Leads on a chip are `lead` lines of `lead-width`. Legs on a through-hole part are tinned wire: a `metal-edge` line of `leg-width` + 0.6 under a `lead` line of `leg-width` − 0.4 (`PartsKit` `wire`). Round every line cap.
- Corners are real mouldings: `radius-board` on boards, `radius-chip` on chips, `radius-bezel` on bezels and glass, `radius-part` on pads and small parts, `radius-detail` on housings. Lenses, knobs, cans and caps are circles.

## Type on parts

- Everything printed on a part is `mono` (`ui-monospace`): `part-number` on a chip, `silk-title` on a board, `module-print` for secondary silk, `pin-label` beside pads and for pin numbers, `readout-digits` on an instrument. `pin-name`, outside a chip's leads, is `currentColor`.
- Sizes are artwork pixels at 1:1; the canvas usually shows parts at 2× to 4×. Nothing printed on a part is smaller than `pin-label` (4px): at the kit's scale (0.1 in = 9.6px) that is 3pt, the floor SparkFun's guide to Fritzing's graphic standards sets. Fritzing's standard size, 5pt, is 6.7px; `part-number` (6.4px) sits just under it and `readout-digits` (7.5px) above.
- Text beside a part (the `value-caption`, the `pin-tooltip`) is the editor's, in `sans`, theme-aware: the artwork never draws a value.

## Pins and labels

- A **pad** is a `pad` circle of `pad-radius` with a `hole` of `hole-radius` ringed in `metal-edge` (the plating; the hole itself shows the canvas, white in the light theme). A **socket** (a board people plug wires into) is a `pitch`-square `plastic-black` housing with a `cavity` square. Drilled holes are `hole`; dark openings that go nowhere (sockets, microphone ports, a jack bore, vents, a relay's pin holes) are `cavity`. An **SMD pad** is a 6.4 × 4.8 `pad` rectangle. A **leg** has no paint of its own: the lead line ends at the pin.
- Label a header pad in `pin-label` on the board's silk, `label-rise` above or `label-drop` below its centre; on a vertical row put labels inside the board.
- Pin names come from the data sheet, in the order printed on the real part. The editor identifies pins by index (`pin-N`), never by name, so the order in `pins` is the contract.
- The editor draws the pin handle, the hover ring (`mark-select`), the wired state (`mark-pin-connected` in `mark-pin-connected-ring`) and the tooltip over the pad. The artwork draws none of them.

## States and hooks

- **Moving parts declare their pivot and travel**, because the sanitizer strips `transform-origin`: every rotating group (`knob`, `horn`, `rotor`, `dial`) carries `data-cx` and `data-cy` for `rotate(deg cx cy)` and `data-angle`; every sliding group (`lever`, `lever-N`, `stick`, `cap`) carries `data-axis` (`x`, `y` or `xy`) and `data-travel` in px.

State is shown with the same palette at a different opacity or position, never with a second palette. The editor animates artwork through stable hooks:

- Give the element that changes an `id`: `lens` and `glow` on an LED (`fill-opacity`), `cap` on a button (`r`, `data-pressed`), `knob` on a pot (`transform`), `lever` on a switch (`x`), `glass` on an LCD, `heat` and `flow-*` on a resistor, `reset-button` on a board (never `reset`, `submit` or any other name of a document or form property: the sanitizer drops those ids). When the editor stores a part it scopes every `id` and exposes the original as `data-svg-id`, so a hook is always written as `id` in the artwork and read as `data-svg-id` by the editor; never write `data-svg-id` yourself, the sanitizer strips it. A state painter sets only `fill`, `stroke`, opacity, `transform` and geometry attributes.
- Onboard LEDs use the ids and attributes the editor already reads: `user-led` for the board's own LED (lit from the solved current), `rgb-led` for an addressable one (filled from its pixel colour), and `data-lit-state="<state>"` with `data-on-fill` and `data-off-fill` for an indicator the part's active state lights (`power`, `tx`, `rx`). `PartsKit.indicator` writes all of them.
- A display window is `id="display"` (and `glass` behind it): the run lays a `<canvas>` over that rectangle, one artwork pixel per panel pixel.
- A logic output lead carries `data-level-pin="<pin index>"`: the editor strokes it `level-high` or `level-low` from the solved voltage.
- A lit lens is opaque with its halo at `opacity-glow` × brightness. Unlit, a top-view lens (an indicator, a dome) is its colour at `opacity-lens-off` over `plastic-grey`; a standing LED's epoxy is its colour at 60% over `plastic-white`, so the lead frame shows through. A graded segment or bar never drops below `opacity-segment-dim`.

## The live layer

The kit's answer to "why does this look different": the circuit is visible in the parts. `signal` (the brand's yellow) means "happening now" and nothing else; the materials stay quiet, so anything yellow on the bench is the thing the person should look at.

- **Flow.** A two-leg part draws `signal` dashes over each leg (`data-live="flow"`, ids `flow-1`, `flow-2`, `flow-a`, `flow-c`), at `opacity-flow` while the run has current through it and 0 otherwise. The dashes are drawn running from pin 1 to pin 2 (anode to cathode on an LED); the editor animates `[data-live="flow"]` with one keyframe on `stroke-dashoffset` (0.7 s, linear, the same cadence as its wires), plays it in reverse when the current runs the other way, and stops it under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Pass `live: { flow: true }` to a generator to draw it on.
- **Heat.** A resistor carries a `band-red` overlay (`id="heat"`) at `opacity-heat` × how close it is to its rating. It warms before it fails, which is the thing a real one does that a drawing never shows.
- **Probe.** The hovered or measured pin gets the probe: a `signal` ring with a `signal-ink` tip (`PartsKit.probe`, `data-live="probe"`) and the `pin-tooltip` beside it. The editor draws it; a generator draws it in a preview with `live: { probe: 'A' }`.
- **Nothing else is yellow.** A lit LED is its lens colour, a HIGH lead is `level-high`, a selection is `mark-select`, a wired pin is `mark-pin-connected`. Keep `signal` for current, probes and the mark on the silk, so it never stops meaning "live".

## Theme, forced colours and contrast

- `mark-select` is indigo (`#6366f1`, `#818cf8` on the dark canvas), a step away from `level-low` blue, so a selected part and a LOW lead never read as the same thing; `canvas-ink-muted` is `#475569` on the light canvas (7.5:1).
- Material tokens are identical in both themes by design. Only `canvas`, `canvas-ink`, `canvas-ink-muted`, `canvas-grid` and `hole` (a drilled hole shows the canvas) change; use `currentColor` wherever a part touches the canvas and it follows.
- In forced-colours mode the editor puts artwork on a white backplate with black `currentColor`; keep that in mind when a part relies on `currentColor` against a dark body (don't).
- Print meets 4.5:1 on its body: `silk` on every dark finish, `silk-dark` on `pcb-white`, `chip-print` on `chip`, `lcd-ink` on `lcd-green`, `lcd-ink-light` on `lcd-blue`, `readout-text` on `readout`, `bread-print` on `bread-body` (measured by `contrast.mjs`). Marks that carry meaning meet 3:1: `cavity` on `pad`, `bread-hole` on `bread-body`; a drilled `hole` reads by its `metal-edge` plating ring, not by contrast with the pad, since it shows the canvas. `level-high` beside `level-low` are the editor's own values and measure 1.07:1, hue alone; the editor also writes `data-level="high|low"` on the lead and the pin tooltip carries the voltage, so colour is not the only carrier. `pad` on `pcb-white` is 1.9:1 and relies on its plated hole centre, as the real board does. Every pair here is measured by `contrast.mjs` (WCAG 2 relative luminance).

## Measured against the published standard

The kit's rules are held against two published standards. The first is the one for breadboard-view part graphics: Fritzing's graphic standards (fritzing.org/fritzings-graphic-standards), SparkFun's guide to them (learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/make-your-own-fritzing-parts/custom-breadboard-svg) and Fritzing's part file format notes (github.com/fritzing/fritzing-app/wiki/2.1-Part-file-format). The second is KiCad's Library Conventions (klc.kicad.org), whose symbol and silkscreen rules give an independent floor for the same measurements. Every value used here (0.1 inch pins, 0.03 inch legs, 5pt print with a 3pt floor, 1.00 mm silkscreen text, 0.10 to 0.15 mm lines, the 100 mil grid) agrees across those sources. `checks/standards.mjs` measures every artwork in the suite, every card's preview and every picture in `images/` against the rules that can be measured.

| Their rule | The kit |
| --- | --- |
| Connector pins a fixed 0.1 inch apart, so the part fits a breadboard | `pinsOnPitch`: every pin a whole number of pitches (9.6px) from the first, on both axes, measured over every artwork and a fuzz of random parts |
| Legs 0.03 inch wide and at least 0.03 inch long | `leg-width` 2.3px inside a 0.6px `metal-edge` outline draws 2.9px, 0.030 inch; a chip's `lead-width` is 2.9px; every standing part's legs reach a whole pitch |
| Text at 5pt, never below 3pt, kept as text | `pin-label` 4px is 3pt at 0.1 inch = 9.6px, and nothing prints smaller; `part-number` 6.4px is 4.8pt; every label is a `<text>` in the kit's `mono` stack, never outlined |
| Explicit values on every attribute, `stroke-width` above all | every stroke is written with its width; the check fails on one without |
| Flat colour, SVG Tiny features only: no gradients, filters or images | flat colour everywhere except the five sheens (`g-dome`, `g-lens`, `g-metal`, `g-glow-<hex>`, `g-hot`), two-stop gradients on curved surfaces only; no filters, patterns or images. A deliberate departure from SVG Tiny, recorded here; `checks/standards.mjs` fails any other `url()` reference |
| Legs grey (8C8C8C), contacts copper (9A916C), their named cable colours | not adopted: those match Fritzing's own library. `lead`, `pad` and `metal` are the kit's materials, held to the contrast floors above |
| OCR A or Droid Sans | not adopted: print is the `mono` stack as a presentation attribute, since SVG sanitizers keep no `<style>` or font files |
| KLC S4.1: pins on a 100 mil (2.54 mm) grid | the same `pinsOnPitch` rule, 100 mil = 9.6px |
| KLC F5.1: silkscreen reference text 1.00 mm; silkscreen lines 0.10 to 0.15 mm, 0.12 nominal | the 4px floor is 1.06 mm at the kit's scale; the finest stroke, `stroke-fine` 0.4px, is 0.106 mm, `stroke-detail` 0.6px is 0.16 mm |

## What ships

A part is production ready when six checks pass, not when it looks right at one zoom:

1. **No overlaps.** `PartsKit.check(artwork)` returns nothing (see the checklist). The kit's own suite runs it over every card's preview, every catalog part and a fuzz of random pin names, counts, finishes and sizes.
2. **It survives a sanitizer.** DeepCircuits stores a part through DOMPurify's SVG profile: no `<style>`, `style`, `<image>` or `<animate>`, ids scoped and exposed as `data-svg-id`, `role` dropped while `aria-label` stays. Draw only what survives it, so nothing else is lost: every element, every attribute, every `data-*` hook, every `id` back as `data-svg-id`.
3. **Print reads.** Every text-on-ground pair in *Theme, forced colours and contrast* meets its floor by `checks/contrast.mjs`.
4. **It carries its inventory.** The parts the real one has, as *Measured from the real part* lists them, are present and tagged (`data-part`): a board has its traces, vias, mark, mount holes and labelled pins; a chip its lot code, notch and pin numbers; an LED its lens, glow and lead frame.
5. **It meets the published standards.** `checks/standards.mjs` passes: pins on the 0.1-inch grid, legs 0.03 inch, no print under 3pt or under KLC's 1.00 mm, no stroke under KLC's 0.10 mm, every stroke with a width, flat colour but the sheens (*Measured against the published standard*). The scripts behind every check here sit at the kit's root; its README says how to run them.
6. **It renders where it is used.** Every card draws in a browser with no console error, wherever the bundle is loaded; the live layer's `pk-flow` animation moves and stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and every card still reads under forced colours. `checks/overlaps.mjs` runs every card's script and checks what it draws. The editor shows a part through the same sanitizer as check 2.

## Families and templates

Build a new part from the nearest template rather than from a blank box:

- **Chip** for any bare IC (DIP, SOIC, TSSOP); **Transistor** for a three-leg part standing in TO-92 or TO-220; **Module** for a breakout with one header row and a part on it (`chip`, `sensor`, `can`, `dual-can`, `dome`), and the photographed breakouts beside it (`dht22Sensor`, `dht11Module`, `ultrasonicRanger`, `bmp180Board`, `pirModule`, `gasModuleTemplate`, `hx711Board`, `microsdModule`, `rtcModule`); **DevBoard** for a microcontroller board with two header rows; **CharacterLcd** and **SevenSegment** for displays; **LogicSymbol** for gates.
- **Resistor**, **Led**, **Capacitor**, **Buzzer** for two-leg parts; **Pushbutton**, **Potentiometer**, **SlideSwitch** for inputs. A new input with a moving part gets an `id` on the part that moves.
- Compose from the primitives (**Pin**, **Header**, **Board**, **Lens**, an indicator, the **Live** layer) when no template fits, and add the result to the kit as a new template with a README that says what the consumer provides.

## The Artwork contract

```ts
interface Artwork { width: number; height: number; svg: string; pins: { name: string; x: number; y: number }[] }
```

The editor places a pin's handle at its `(x, y)` and knows each pin by its place in `pins`, so the order is the contract. Keep the generator free of DOM and host code so it runs in Node, and say on its card when an attribute changes the pins (a digit count, a chain length), since a host must then rebuild the handles.

## Checklist for a new part

1. Pins on the pitch (`pinsOnPitch` passes) and named from the data sheet, in the printed order.
2. Only material tokens on the body; `currentColor` for anything on the canvas; no gradients but the kit's five sheens (`g-dome`, `g-lens`, `g-metal`, `g-hot`, `g-glow-<hex>`), no filters, `<style>` or `style=""`.
3. One edge stroke per body; strokes, radii and text sizes from the scales.
4. State through `id` hooks, `id="display"` or `data-level-pin`, shown by opacity or position; current through the live layer (`flow`, `heat`), and `signal` used for nothing else.
5. `role="img"` and an `aria-label` with the state in words.
6. Print at 4.5:1 on its body; meaningful marks at 3:1.
7. No vendor art, no vendor name.
8. A card (`generators/<Name>/preview.html`) and a README that says what the consumer provides.
9. `PartsKit.check(artwork)` returns nothing: no label on another label or on a part, no part on another part, nothing outside the box. Every drawn part is wrapped in a `data-part` group so the check can see it; `body` and `board` are grounds the others may sit on.
10. `checks/standards.mjs` passes with the new part in the suite: no print under `pin-label` (4px), every stroke with its width, pins on the grid, flat colour but the sheens.
